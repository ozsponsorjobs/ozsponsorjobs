482 vs 186 vs 494: Which Australian Work Visa Fits You?

Australia has three main employer-sponsored work visas. They look similar — all need an employer to nominate you — but they differ on permanence, experience, age, salary and where you can live. Here is how to choose.

Updated: November 2026 Author: OZ Sponsor Jobs Editorial Team Reviewed by: Australian Migration Policy Specialists Legal Basis: Migration Act 1958; Migration Regulations 1994 (Parts 482, 186, 494)
✔ Direct Answer (BLUF)

The 482 is the fast entry route: a temporary visa for up to four years, one year of experience, IELTS 5.0, no age limit, and permanent residency after two years with your sponsor. The 186 is permanent from day one, but harder to get: three years of experience, a skills assessment, under 45, and IELTS 6.0. The 494 is a five-year provisional visa for regional Australia only, with a path to the permanent 191 after three years. All three need a sponsoring employer and a salary of at least $79,423 a year (for nominations lodged from 1 July 2026).

1. The Three Visas at a Glance

All three visas are employer-sponsored: an Australian employer nominates you for a specific job, and you apply for the visa. The differences are what matters.

The salary floor is the same dollar figure for all three — $79,423 a year for nominations lodged between 1 July 2026 and 30 June 2027 — but it has different names: the Core Skills Income Threshold (CSIT) for the 482 and 186, and the Temporary Skilled Migration Income Threshold (TSMIT) for the 494. Your salary must also be at least the market rate for the role in all three cases. The threshold is explained in CSIT Explained.

2. The 482: Fast Entry

The Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482) is Australia’s main temporary work visa. Since 7 December 2024 it has replaced the old Temporary Skill Shortage visa. It lets an approved Standard Business Sponsor employ you for up to four years, anywhere in Australia, in an occupation on the Core Skills Occupation List (456 occupations). You need only 12 months of relevant experience, English at IELTS 5.0 in each component, and no skills assessment in most cases. There is no age limit.

The 482 is the easiest of the three to enter, and it is the most common first step. Its main limitation is that it is temporary and tied to your sponsor — you must work for your sponsoring employer in your nominated occupation. Since July 2024, if you leave your sponsor you have up to 180 days to find a new one, and you can work in any occupation during that period. After two years of full-time work for your sponsor, you can move to permanent residency through the 186 Temporary Residence Transition stream. The full guide is How to Find a 482 Visa Sponsorship Job in Australia (2026 Guide).

3. The 186: Permanent from Day One

The Employer Nomination Scheme (subclass 186) is the only one of the three that is permanent from the day it is granted. You can live and work anywhere in Australia, with no condition tying you to your sponsor after grant, and you can include your partner and dependent children. That permanence is why it is also the hardest to get.

The Direct Entry stream is for people applying from outside Australia, or from inside without a 482 history. Your occupation must be on the Core Skills Occupation List, you need three years of relevant experience, a positive skills assessment (unless exempt), to be under 45, and Competent English — IELTS 6.0 in each component (or PTE Academic 50 in each). The Temporary Residence Transition (TRT) stream is for people who have held a 482 (or 457) visa and worked full-time for their nominating employer in their nominated occupation for two years in the last three years. TRT has no occupation list and no mandatory skills assessment, but the age and English requirements still apply. The third stream, Labour Agreement, covers employers with a formal agreement with the Government.

Two things surprise applicants. First, the employer does not need to be an approved Standard Business Sponsor for a 186 — the nomination is direct. Second, the age limit has exemptions: academics, scientists and researchers, regional medical practitioners, and high-income earners whose salary is at least the Fair Work High Income Threshold of $175,000 (2025–26) can be nominated over 45. You must lodge your 186 application within six months of the nomination being approved.

4. The 494: Regional Route

The Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (Provisional) visa (subclass 494) is a five-year provisional visa for regional Australia — everywhere except Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane. It exists because regional employers struggle most to fill roles, and the trade-off is clear: you get a visa and a path to permanent residency, but you must live, work and study only in a designated regional area (condition 8579) while you hold it.

The Employer Sponsored stream requires three years of full-time relevant work, a positive skills assessment (unless exempt), to be under 45, and Competent English. The occupation list is wider than the 482’s: the 494 uses the Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List (MLTSSL), the Short-term Skilled Occupation List (STSOL) and the Regional Occupation List (ROL) — around 650 occupations in total — so some occupations that are not on the CSOL are available through the 494. The salary floor is the TSMIT: $79,423 a year for nominations lodged between 1 July 2026 and 30 June 2027, plus the market rate. A Labour Agreement stream also exists for employers with a formal agreement, including Designated Area Migration Agreements (DAMAs). Our DAMA hub lists the regional agreements.

The 494’s advantage is its permanent residency pathway: after holding the visa for three years and complying with the regional condition, you can apply for the subclass 191 permanent visa. The 191 has no minimum income requirement — an old figure of around $53,900 that still circulates online does not apply. You do need Australian Taxation Office notices of assessment for three income years out of the five. The 191 visa application charge is $630 (from 1 July 2026).

5. Eligibility Side by Side

The three visas sort applicants by experience, English and age. Work through the list in order — the first test you fail decides which visa is open to you.

  • Experience. One year of relevant work opens the 482. Three years opens the 186 Direct Entry and the 494. Two years with the same sponsor opens the 186 TRT route.
  • English. IELTS 5.0 in each component is enough for the 482. The 186 and 494 need Competent English: IELTS 6.0 in each component (or PTE Academic 50 in each).
  • Age. The 482 has no age limit. The 186 and 494 require you to be under 45, with exemptions for academics, scientists and researchers, regional medical practitioners, and high-income earners (at least the $175,000 Fair Work High Income Threshold).
  • Occupation. The 482 and 186 Direct Entry use the Core Skills Occupation List (456 occupations). The 494 uses MLTSSL, STSOL and ROL (around 650 occupations). The 186 TRT stream has no occupation list at all. The lists are explained in Skilled Occupation Lists Explained.
  • Skills assessment. Usually not needed for the 482. Required for the 186 Direct Entry and the 494 unless you are exempt. Not required for the 186 TRT stream.
  • Location. The 482 and 186 let you live anywhere in Australia. The 494 requires you to live, work and study only in a regional area.

If you are unsure where you stand, the eligibility calculator on this site can help you work through the basic checks, and the sponsors directory lists employers drawn from live job listings (their sponsorship status is not verified — check each employer yourself, as explained in How to Check If an Australian Employer Is a Genuine Standard Business Sponsor).

The sponsorship, nomination and levy costs belong to the employer by law and cannot be recovered from you. The visa application charge is usually paid by the worker. Note the 186 also has a second instalment for applicants with non-functional English: $9,800 for the main applicant and $4,890 for other applicants. On top of the government charges, budget for the English test, any skills assessment, health examinations and police certificates, which are separate costs. And on top of your salary, your employer must pay superannuation at 12% since 1 July 2025.

7. The Paths to Permanent Residency

Only the 186 is permanent on grant. The other two are designed to lead to permanent residency — and the routes are different enough to matter when you choose.

  • 482 → 186 TRT. After two years of full-time work for your sponsoring employer in your nominated occupation, your employer can nominate you for the 186 Temporary Residence Transition stream. No occupation list, no mandatory skills assessment — but you must still be under 45 (unless exempt) and meet Competent English, and your salary must be at least the CSIT plus the market rate.
  • 494 → 191. After holding the 494 for three years and complying with the regional residence condition, you apply for the 191 permanent visa. There is no minimum income requirement, but you need ATO notices of assessment for three income years out of five. The 191 application charge is $630.
  • 186 Direct Entry. Permanent immediately, with no further step — but you must meet the full Direct Entry requirements up front: CSOL occupation, three years of experience, skills assessment, under 45, Competent English.

In practice, the most common journey is 482 first, then 186 TRT after two years — the lowest-barrier entry and the most flexible route.

8. Which One Fits You?

Match your situation to the visa, not the other way around.

  • You have one year of experience and IELTS 5.0. The 482 is your route. It is the only one of the three you currently qualify for.
  • You have three years of experience, a skills assessment and IELTS 6.0, and you are under 45. The 186 Direct Entry is the best prize: permanent residency immediately, no sponsor tie after grant, and you can live anywhere.
  • You are already on a 482 and have worked for your sponsor for two years. Ask your employer about the 186 TRT stream. It is the simplest permanent residency application you will ever make.
  • Your occupation is not on the CSOL, but it is on the MLTSSL, STSOL or ROL. The 494 is your route — provided you are willing to live regionally for at least three years.
  • You are over 45. The 482 has no age limit, so it is open to you. The 186 and 494 are closed unless you qualify for an age exemption.
  • You want to live in Sydney, Melbourne or Brisbane. The 494 is not an option. Choose between the 482 and the 186.
  • You want the fastest possible entry to Australia. The 482: the lowest experience and English requirements, and most applicants need no skills assessment.
