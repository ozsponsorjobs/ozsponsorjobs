How to Find a 482 Visa Sponsorship Job in Australia (2026 Guide)

The Skills in Demand (subclass 482) visa is Australia’s main employer-sponsored work visa. Here is how it works in 2026, what you need to qualify, where sponsoring employers actually hire, and how to avoid the scams that target job seekers.

Updated: November 2026 Author: OZ Sponsor Jobs Editorial Team Reviewed by: Australian Migration Policy Specialists Legal Basis: Migration Act 1958; Migration Regulations 1994 (Part 482)
✔ Direct Answer (BLUF)

The 482 visa — now called the Skills in Demand visa — lets an approved Australian employer sponsor you for up to four years. You need a job offer from an approved sponsor, an occupation on the Core Skills Occupation List (456 occupations), at least 12 months of relevant experience, and English at IELTS 5.0 in each component. Your salary must be at least the Core Skills Income Threshold of AUD $79,423 a year (for nominations lodged from 1 July 2026) and at least the market rate for the role. The visa application charge is AUD $4,015.

1. What the 482 Visa Is

The Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482) is a temporary employer-sponsored work visa. It lets an approved Australian employer sponsor a skilled worker from overseas to fill a role it cannot fill locally, for up to four years. It is tied to one employer and one occupation, but it is also the most common stepping stone to Australian permanent residency: after two years with your sponsor you can move to the subclass 186 visa.

The visa changed on 7 December 2024, when the old Temporary Skill Shortage (TSS) visa became the Skills in Demand visa. The subclass number stayed 482, but the occupation lists were replaced by the Core Skills Occupation List, the experience requirement dropped from two years to one, and visa holders gained up to 180 days to find a new sponsor.

Getting a 482 visa is a three-step process: the employer becomes an approved Standard Business Sponsor (or already is one), then nominates you for a specific position, and you lodge your visa application.

2. The Three Streams

The 482 has three streams. Most job seekers will use Core Skills.

Stream Occupation list Salary floor Max stay
Core Skills Core Skills Occupation List (456 occupations) CSIT $79,423 / yr Up to 4 years
Specialist Skills Any occupation in ANZSCO Major Groups 1, 2, 4, 5 or 6 (no trades, machinery operators, drivers or labourers) SSIT $146,576 / yr Up to 4 years
Labour Agreement As set out in the employer’s agreement As set out in the agreement Up to 4 years

Core Skills is the mainstream route: your occupation must be on the Core Skills Occupation List, matched by ANZSCO code, and your salary must clear the CSIT. Specialist Skills is for highly paid specialists in the professional, managerial, community-service, clerical/administrative or sales major groups, earning at least $146,576 a year. Labour Agreement covers employers with a formal agreement with the Australian Government, including DAMAs for regional areas. There is no age limit for any 482 stream.

3. Do You Qualify?

Check these four requirements before you spend months applying: if any one fails, the employer cannot sponsor you for that role.

Your occupation must be on the list

For the Core Skills stream, your nominated occupation must be on the Core Skills Occupation List, matched by its ANZSCO code — not by job title alone. The code, not the job title, is what the Department checks. Search the official skilled occupation list on the Home Affairs website using your exact occupation and confirm the CSOL entry, including any caveat. If your occupation is not on the CSOL, the Core Skills stream is closed to you for that role. Check the basic requirements with the eligibility calculator on this site.

At least 12 months of relevant experience

Since the Skills in Demand reforms, the work experience requirement for the 482 is one year, not two. You must have at least 12 months of full-time equivalent work in the nominated occupation or a related field, gained within the five years before you apply. Part-time and casual work counts if it adds up to the full-time equivalent. The comparison is between duties, not job titles: experience counts when the tasks were the same as, or closely related to, the duties of the nominated occupation.

English: IELTS 5.0 in each component

For the Core Skills and Specialist Skills streams you need at least IELTS 5.0 in each of listening, reading, writing and speaking, or the equivalent in another accepted test (for example, PTE Academic 33 in listening, 36 in reading, 29 in writing and 24 in speaking for tests taken on or after 13 September 2025). You are exempt if you are a citizen and passport holder of the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada, New Zealand or Ireland, or if you completed at least five years of full-time secondary or higher study taught in English.

Skills assessment: usually not needed

Most 482 applicants do not need a formal skills assessment. A mandatory assessment applies only if you are applying in the Core Skills or Specialist Skills stream, you hold a passport from a listed country, and your occupation is one of 24 listed occupations — mostly trades assessed by Trades Recognition Australia.

4. The Salary Floor: CSIT $79,423

Every 482 nomination has a salary floor. For the Core Skills stream it is called the Core Skills Income Threshold (CSIT). For nominations lodged between 1 July 2026 and 30 June 2027 the CSIT is AUD $79,423 a year. Your guaranteed annual earnings must be at least that amount. The figure is indexed every 1 July: it was $73,150 at launch on 7 December 2024 and $76,515 from 1 July 2025. The threshold that applies to you is the one in force on the day your nomination is lodged.

The CSIT is only half of the salary test. Your pay must also be at least the annual market salary rate — what an equivalent Australian worker earns for the same job in the same location. The Specialist Skills stream has its own, higher threshold: the Specialist Skills Income Threshold (SSIT) of AUD $146,576 a year for nominations lodged between 1 July 2026 and 30 June 2027. See CSIT Explained for the full detail.

5. Where Sponsoring Employers Actually Hire

Most sponsored jobs are never advertised with the words “visa sponsorship available”. Employers hire for the role first and sponsor second, so target employers that already sponsor.

Start with sponsorship-aware job boards

The major Australian job boards — SEEK, Indeed and LinkedIn — all let you filter for visa sponsorship. Search for your occupation plus “visa sponsorship” or “482”. Browse current roles on this site by visa subclass: 482 sponsorship jobs, 186 jobs and 494 regional jobs.

Use sponsor directories as a target list

A sponsor directory tells you which employers have sponsored before. Our sponsors directory lists Australian employers drawn from live job listings. It is built from job advertisements, and we do not verify any employer’s sponsorship status — each profile honestly states that its Standard Business Sponsor status is not verified. Use it as a starting list, then verify the employer yourself on ABN Lookup and its careers page before you apply. How to do that properly is covered in How to Check If an Australian Employer Is a Genuine Standard Business Sponsor.

Approach employers directly

Direct outreach works well in shortage occupations. Contact the hiring manager or HR team at 20 to 30 employers in your field with a short, specific message: your occupation and ANZSCO code, your years of experience, your English result, and the fact that you need a 482 nomination. Never pay an agent or an employer for a job or a sponsorship — that is a scam, and it is unlawful for a sponsor to pass its sponsorship costs on to you.

Look regionally, not just at Sydney and Melbourne

The largest cities have the most jobs but also the most competition. Regional Australia — everywhere outside Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane — has persistent shortages in health, aged care, hospitality, agriculture and trades. The 494 regional visa is designed for exactly these roles, and Designated Area Migration Agreements can cover occupations not on the CSOL. Our DAMA hub explains the regional agreements and lists the areas that have them.

6. Your Step-by-Step Plan

Each step is simple on its own; the delays come from doing them in the wrong order.

  1. Confirm your occupation. Find your ANZSCO code and check it against the Core Skills Occupation List, including any caveat.
  2. Prove your English. Book an accepted English test now if you are not exempt.
  3. Check whether you need a skills assessment. Most applicants do not. If your occupation and passport put you in the 24-occupation list, start the assessment early.
  4. Prepare an Australian-style CV. Two to four pages, duties and achievements (not just job titles), Australian spelling, and your ANZSCO occupation named clearly.
  5. Apply where sponsors hire. Use sponsorship filters on the major boards, browse the jobs board and the sponsors directory, and contact employers directly in your occupation.
  6. Secure the offer in writing. Confirm the salary in writing and check it against the CSIT and the market rate.
  7. Let the employer run sponsorship and nomination. The employer applies for sponsorship (if it is not already approved), then lodges the nomination for your position. The nomination includes labour market testing: the employer must have advertised the role for at least 28 days, within the four months before lodging.
  8. Lodge your visa application. Once the nomination is approved, you lodge your 482 application in ImmiAccount with your supporting documents. The visa application charge for the main applicant is AUD $4,015.

If you are unsure about any step, talk to a registered migration agent — you can confirm an agent is registered on the MARA website — rather than relying on social media advice.

The sponsorship, nomination and levy costs belong to the employer by law and cannot be recovered from you. If an employer or agent asks you to pay any of them, walk away.

8. Red Flags and Scams

Sponsorship scams target job seekers because the process is complicated and the prize is valuable. Our guide How to Check If an Australian Employer Is a Genuine Standard Business Sponsor covers the checks in detail.

  • You are asked to pay for the job or the sponsorship. Sponsorship, nomination and levy costs are the employer’s by law. Any request for payment for a job offer, a sponsorship or a nomination is a scam.
  • There is no real business. No ABN, no website, no physical address, no presence on LinkedIn or Google Maps, and contact only through WhatsApp or Telegram. Check the legal business name on ABN Lookup before you engage further.
  • The salary is below the floor. A 482 Core Skills offer below $79,423 a year (for nominations lodged from 1 July 2026) cannot be approved.
  • A visa is “guaranteed”. Nobody can guarantee a visa outcome. Only the Department of Home Affairs decides, and only after sponsorship, nomination and your application have all been assessed.
  • The occupation does not match your experience. If the employer wants to nominate you in an occupation you have never worked in, the nomination will fail.
  • Everything happens outside ImmiAccount. Australian visa applications are lodged in ImmiAccount, the Department’s official system. A “visa” that cannot be checked in VEVO (Visa Entitlement Verification Online) is not a visa.

If something feels wrong, stop and verify before you pay anything or share identity documents.

9. After You Arrive

Your 482 visa requires you to work only for your sponsoring employer (or an associated entity) and only in your nominated occupation, unless an exemption applies. Since July 2024, a 482 holder who stops working for their sponsor has up to 180 days to find a new sponsor, and can work in any occupation during that period while looking.

Your partner and dependent children can be included, and partners on a 482 visa have unrestricted work rights. After two years of full-time work for your sponsoring employer in your nominated occupation, you become eligible for the Temporary Residence Transition stream of the subclass 186 — permanent residency through the same employer.
